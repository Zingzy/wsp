# The computer and its processes

{/* Written from PANES in apps/web/src/panes.ts, apps/web/src/components/machine/MachineSurface.tsx, apps/web/src/components/procs/, apps/web/src/machine/live.ts. */}

Two panes show the computer a thread runs on. The Computer pane shows its load, memory and disk. The Processes pane lists what runs on it, and you can stop a process from there. With no thread open, both panes show the computer you sit at.

To open either one, click **Add a panel**, the **+** at the top of the right panel, and pick **Computer** or **Processes**. If the right panel is empty, pick it from the list there. In that menu or that list, `M` picks Computer and `P` picks Processes.

## The Computer pane

The pane draws three lines over the last two minutes:

- **Load**, the load average over one minute.
- **Memory**, how much is in use, out of the total.
- **Disk**, how much is in use, out of the total.

The memory and disk lines turn to a warning color at 70% and to a danger color at 90%.

Under the lines, the pane names the computer, its cores and its memory. For a thread, it also names the thread's state, where it runs and the project folder. If the computer does not answer, the lines say so in place of a value.

## The Processes pane

The pane is one table with four columns: `pid`, `cpu`, `mem` and `command`. Child processes sit under their parent, and the heaviest process comes first.

- Click the `cpu` or `mem` header to sort by it.
- Type in **filter** to keep only the rows that match.

A row marked `agent` is a thread's agent, `terminal` is a shell in a terminal pane, and `wsp` is wsp itself. On your own computers, the thread's processes come first under the thread's title, and the rest sit under **Everything else**.

### Read one process

Click a row, or select it and press `Enter`, to open it. The pane shows the process's user, state, start time, threads, working folder, the TCP ports it listens on, its children and its full command.

### Stop a process

1. Open the row and click **kill**.
2. Within 2 seconds, click **confirm TERM**. wsp sends the process `SIGTERM`.
3. If the process is still there after 5 seconds, click **kill -9**, then **confirm KILL**. wsp sends `SIGKILL`.

There is no dialog. The second click is the confirmation.

### When the daemon stops

The daemon is the wsp program on each computer that runs terminals and reads files for the app. If it stops, the pane says so. Where wsp can start it again, the pane shows **Start again**.
