# The switcher and the palette

{/* Written from apps/web/src/keybindingDefaults.ts, apps/web/src/shell/ (shellCommands.ts, threadHistory.ts, workspaceSwitcher.ts), apps/web/src/components/palette/, apps/web/src/keybindings.ts. */}

Three ways move you between threads with the keyboard. The switcher steps through the threads you opened last. The palette searches threads and actions. The jump keys open a row of the palette's Tasks group by its number.

In the chords below, `mod` is `Cmd` on a Mac and `Ctrl` elsewhere. You can change every chord in Settings > Keybindings. The [keyboard shortcuts](/features/shortcuts) page lists them all.

## The switcher

Hold `Ctrl` and press `Tab` to open the switcher. Each card shows a thread: a preview of its page, its title, its agent, its computer and its state.

<Image src="/shots/switcher.webp" alt="The switcher held open over five threads." />

1. Hold `Ctrl` and press `Tab`. The next card is picked.
2. Press `Tab` again to move on, or `Shift+Tab` to move back.
3. Let go of `Ctrl` to open the picked thread.

To close the switcher without a move, press `Esc`.

A quick `Ctrl+Tab` takes you back to the thread you had open before. That makes it easy to go back and forth between two threads.

The switcher lists up to six threads. The open thread is first, then the others by when you last opened them, newest first. Threads you never opened come last, in sidebar order. It leaves out settled threads.

`mod+alt+left` and `mod+alt+right` open the same switcher. `mod+alt+up` and `mod+alt+down` open it over the threads of the open project only.

When the right panel has more than one tab and holds focus, `Ctrl+Tab` steps through the panel's tabs instead. A focused terminal keeps `Ctrl+Tab` for itself.

## The palette

Press `mod+k` or `mod+shift+p`, or click **Search** at the top of the sidebar, to open the palette.

<Image src="/shots/docs-palette.webp" alt="The palette with the word settle typed." />

Before you type, the palette shows three groups:

- **Actions**: things to do, such as **New thread**, **Add a project**, **Settings** and **Report a bug**, and the actions of the open project.
- **Tasks**: one row for each project folder that has a thread, and one for each worktree. Each row shows its jump key.
- **Recent threads**: the 12 threads started last.

When you type, it searches the actions, the Tasks rows, the titles of your threads, and the words in their messages. A match in a message shows under **In messages**, with the words around it.

To search the actions only, start with `>`.

An action that cannot run now shows grey, with the reason in place of its description.

The palette does not search files. To find a file, press `mod+p`. See [Files](/features/files).

## Jump to a row

`mod+1` to `mod+9` open the rows of the palette's **Tasks** group by their number. `mod+1` is the first row. The rows are in the order in which wsp made them, oldest first, and the order does not change while you work. A project folder with no thread has no row, and each worktree has a row of its own. If there is no row at that number, nothing happens.

The sidebar shows threads, not these rows, so the numbers do not match the sidebar. Open the palette to see which row has which number.

## Go to the next thread that needs you

Press `mod+alt+u` to open the next thread that waits for you. A thread waits for you when it asks a question or a permission. It also waits for you when it finished or failed, and no window has shown that yet. It goes down the sidebar from the open thread and starts again at the top. If no thread waits for you, nothing happens.

## In a browser tab

A browser keeps some chords for itself: `Ctrl+Tab`, `mod` with a number, `mod+t`, and on a Mac `Cmd+Option` with the arrows. In a browser tab, use the palette or the sidebar instead.
