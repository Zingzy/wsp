# The desktop app on a Mac

{/* Written from apps/desktop/src/ (tray.ts, quit.ts, awake.ts, needs-you.ts, self-update.ts, get-bundle.ts), the General rows in apps/web/src/settings/format.ts, apps/web/src/settings/about.tsx, apps/web/src/notices/hostNotices.ts, apps/desktop/src/main.ts. */}

The wsp app on a Mac is the same app you get in a browser tab, in a window of its own. It adds a menu bar menu and notifications, keeps the Mac awake while threads work, and updates itself. wsp runs in the background, so closing the window or quitting the app does not stop your threads unless you ask it to.

## The menu bar

The app stays in the menu bar when you close its window. The icon shows how many threads work, and turns into a question mark while a thread waits for you.

The menu lists each thread that works, waits or failed, with **Open** and **Stop**. A thread on a permission prompt also has **Allow** and **Deny**, so you can answer from the menu. If wsp is not running, the menu offers **Start wsp**.

## Notifications and sounds

While the wsp window does not have focus, the app tells you when something happens. **Settings > General > Notifications** has two rows for threads:

| Row | When | Default |
|---|---|---|
| When a thread needs you | A question, a permission prompt or a sign-in. | Notify and sound |
| When a thread finishes | A thread that you or the command line started finishes or fails. | Notify |

Each row takes **Off**, **Notify**, **Sound** or **Notify and sound**. A thread that an agent started reports to that agent, not to you.

Click a notification to open what it is about. The **When a plan window runs low** switch tells you when an account nears its plan limit. See [Usage](/features/usage).

## Keep the Mac awake

While a thread works on your Mac, the app keeps the Mac from going to sleep. The display can still sleep. After you quit the app, the Mac can sleep even if threads go on. To turn this off, use **Keep *name* awake** in **Settings > General**.

## Quit

Click **Quit wsp** in the menu, or press `Cmd+Q`. What happens next is set by **When you quit** in **Settings > General**:

| Choice | What a quit does |
|---|---|
| Ask each time | Asks **Quit wsp?** with **Quit**, **Quit and stop wsp** and **Cancel**. This is the default. |
| Keep threads running | Quits the app. wsp keeps running, so threads go on and the `wsp` command still answers. |
| Stop wsp too | Stops the threads on your Mac, then stops wsp, then quits. |

**Start wsp at login** starts wsp in the background when you log in.

## Updates

**Settings > General > Version** shows the version of wsp, and says when a newer one is out.

If the app is in your Applications folder, it updates itself:

1. The app downloads a new release in the background and checks it.
2. A notice says **wsp *version* is ready to install**.
3. Click **Restart**. Running threads go on. Open terminals close.

Click **Later** to skip it for now. To update later, click **Get *version*** in the Version card, then **Restart to update**.

If the app runs from a disk image, it cannot replace itself. Move it to Applications and open it again. Until then, **Get *version*** downloads the disk image, and **Quit and open** opens it so you can drag the new app over the old one.

The builds are not signed with an Apple Developer ID. The first time you open the app, macOS asks you to allow it. Click **Open Anyway** in **System Settings > Privacy & Security**.
